In trading, a Liquidity Sweep occurs when price temporarily moves beyond an obvious swing high or swing low to trigger stop-loss orders and pending liquidity, but then quickly returns and closes back inside the previous range.

For our trading setup, the logic should be:

Bullish Liquidity Sweep

A bullish liquidity sweep occurs when:

Price moves below a previous confirmed Swing Low or Support level.
The candle forms a wick below that level.
The candle then closes back above the Swing Low.
Preferably, the sweep occurs with strong or above-average volume.
It should happen near a valid support or BKP area.
A bullish reversal candlestick pattern provides additional confirmation.

When these conditions are satisfied, the chart can display:

SWEEP L

This indicates that liquidity below the previous low has been taken and price has rejected the lower level.

Bearish Liquidity Sweep

A bearish liquidity sweep occurs when:

Price moves above a previous confirmed Swing High or Resistance level.
The candle forms a wick above that level.
The candle then closes back below the Swing High.
Preferably, the sweep occurs with strong or above-average volume.
It should happen near a valid resistance or BKT area.
A bearish reversal candlestick pattern provides additional confirmation.

When these conditions are satisfied, the chart can display:

SWEEP H

This indicates that liquidity above the previous high has been taken and price has rejected the higher level.

Liquidity Sweep vs Genuine Breakout

A Liquidity Sweep means:

Level Break → Wick Outside the Level → Candle Closes Back Inside

This represents rejection and may indicate a possible reversal.

A Genuine Breakout or Liquidity Clearance means:

Level Break → Candle Closes Outside the Level → Follow-through Continues Outside

This means the market has genuinely broken the liquidity level rather than only sweeping it.

For a confirmed upside break:

LIQ H CLEARED

For a confirmed downside break:

LIQ L CLEARED

The Pine Script logic can therefore follow these basic conditions:

Bullish Sweep
Low < Previous Confirmed Dow Swing Low
AND
Close > Previous Swing Low

→ SWEEP L

Bearish Sweep
High > Previous Confirmed Dow Swing High
AND
Close < Previous Swing High

→ SWEEP H

A liquidity sweep should not be treated as a standalone BUY or SELL signal. It should act as a confirmation or setup-strength upgrade for existing setups such as Momentum, Swing, FBO/FBD, Double Bottom/Double Top, or Triangle setups.

Also, every wick beyond a swing level should not automatically be considered a liquidity sweep. The sweep should preferably be validated using confirmed Dow Theory swings, Support/Resistance, candle-close confirmation, volume, and reversal candlestick context.
